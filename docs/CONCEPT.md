# Artifice

- **Date:** 2026-11-04. **Pages:** [open questions](https://claude.ai/artifact/TPWNRT6HAxtDPSnwhRNGtM), [concepts](https://claude.ai/artifact/7Rvk1HAdvK1EcWVgYDkRNP), [round 1](https://claude.ai/artifact/J2wvcWkCKBkBYwCBTMVs2J), [round 2](https://claude.ai/artifact/Skf9Aids5RjZPmhExmYpkD). **Agent cost:** $1.12 measured (research $0.67, critique $0.45)

## Pitch

You run a fantasy smithy with a shop. You forge, alloy and pour by hand, and your own precision sets each item's quality grade. Customers commission work and caravans bring scarce ore. Morgan's itch: "I just always have loved crafting and nothing has ever really scratched the itch of being a crafter properly."

## Who it's for

Players who love crafting and want it to be the whole game, not a side menu, with a shopkeeper side. Phone or PC.

## Core loop or idea

1. A customer commissions an item with specific needs ("a blade that won't crack in frost", "a cheap horseshoe, today").
2. You pick materials. Their quality varies by region and batch, so you judge, test and refine them (smelt out impurities, choose an alloy) as part of the craft.
3. You craft by hand: heating, hammering, pouring. Your timing and precision set the grade. Rarity = material quality + craft grade.
4. You deliver and price the item. Money and reputation pay for caravans: you choose a route and its risk to get scarce ore and patterns, with no waiting timers.

## What it isn't

- "Just a sim." No stock-management simulation in the shop.
- Easy. Materials and patterns that are "too easy to get" would be boring (Morgan). Scarcity comes from choices about risk and money, not grind.
- Tier-only quality like Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2, where crafting "didn't really have much by way of quality of the end product except like tiers" and was "fairly easy".
- An alloy lab. "Ultimately it is a smithy." Discovering materials is one part of the craft, not the core.
- Not a recreation of any existing game.

## Concepts dropped

- **The Guild Trials** (crafting to judged briefs to climb a guild): cut by Morgan. Its useful part, requests with competing needs, lives on in the customers' commissions.
- **The Alloy Book** (discovering hidden material properties as the core): merged into the chosen concept, at a smaller size. Morgan: it "gets slightly away from the feeling of being a real crafter".
- **The Shop and the Caravan**: merged into the chosen concept as its frame.
- **The Wandering Artisan** (the crafter travels and gathers in person): parked. It needs a world and danger before gathering is fun, the biggest scope of the five. Going on expeditions yourself may come back as a later layer.
- **The Guild Quartermaster** (equip adventurers whose survival depends on your gear): parked. Its payoff happens off-screen and can feel like dice. Caravan guards whose results depend on your gear may come back as a later layer.

## Kill criteria

- The hands-on smithing step isn't fun on its own: in the prototype, Morgan doesn't want to forge another item after 10 crafts without the shop or caravans attached.
- Precision doesn't show: a careful craft and a sloppy one with the same materials don't produce a clearly different grade that the player can see and explain.
- The craft gets solved: once a player knows the best sequence, it works every time and material variation no longer changes what they do.
- The shop or caravans take more build time than the smithing before the smithing feels good (the critique's top risk for this concept).
- Grade and rarity don't change what customers pay or say, so precision has no point.

## Open questions

- Art style: "a slightly pixelated graphics art style but I'm not sure on that yet."
- Phone, PC or both first: affects the controls for hands-on crafting (touch vs mouse).
- Making and selling your own items, not only commissions: wanted later, "not necessary at start".
- Whether it's solo work: Morgan gave 20–40 hours a week, low art and tool cost, and about $50 to start, but didn't say whether anyone else helps.

## Design brief

Start `planning-interview` from this.

**Settled, don't reopen:** fantasy setting; a smithy with a shop; hands-on crafting where the player's precision sets the grade; material quality varies and is judged and refined as part of the craft; rarity = material quality + craft grade; customers commission items with needs; caravans bring scarce ore and patterns through route-and-risk choices, not timers; materials and patterns are hard to get; trading is in; smithing is the only profession in the first prototype; the wandering-artisan and quartermaster ideas are parked; the first goal is a prototype Morgan enjoys; limits are 20–40 hours a week, low art and tool cost, about $50 to start.

**Open for design:**
- The smithing interaction itself: which steps (smelt, alloy, heat, hammer, quench, pour into a mould, finish), and what precision means in each (timing, temperature, placement).
- How the grade is calculated and shown, and how material quality and craft grade combine into a rarity score.
- The material model: which properties exist, how they vary, and how the player judges and refines them.
- The commission format and how grade changes price and reputation.
- How caravans, routes and risk work, and what patterns are and unlock.
- Platform and controls first (phone touch or PC mouse), and the art style.
- What the first playable prototype contains, and how Morgan will judge that it's enjoyable.

**Later, not in the prototype:** more professions with their own hands-on crafts and trade between them; selling your own items outside commissions; expeditions in person; caravan guards whose results depend on your gear.
